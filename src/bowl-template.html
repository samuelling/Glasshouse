How it's modeled

Bowl. This is the same thin-shell finite-element solve as the glass, applied to a hand-hammered bowl. The meridian runs all the way to the axis, where the shell closes. The wall thickens into a rolled lip at the rim and toward the hammered base, and the bowl sits free on a cushion instead of being clamped to a stem. The partials come out inharmonic, as in real bowls: roughly 1 : 2.7 : 4.9 : 7.5 for the Thadobati. A 1.25 kg, Ø18 cm bell-bronze bowl sings near 290 Hz. "Antique Himalayan alloy" uses the effective modulus Terwagne & Bush measured on real bowls (≈77 GPa at ≈8.9 g/cm³).

Puja. The stick presses on the outside wall about 1.5 cm below the rim. Contact is unilateral: a stiff spring-dashpot that can only push. The stick has mass, and your hand pushes it with the set force. Friction between stick and bronze weakens with sliding speed and is solved implicitly every 10 µs, using the normal force the contact produces at that instant. A leather wrap is soft and lossy, so it stays in contact. Bare wood is stiff, so once the wall's motion exceeds the stick's tiny static indentation, the stick bounces off every cycle. That is the rattle you get when you rub a loud bowl with a wooden stick.

Outside vs. inside. Real bowls only sing when rubbed on the outside (Debut & Antunes). The model shows the same thing without being told: rubbed from inside, the radial–tangential coupling of the shell mode removes energy instead of feeding it, so the bowl only scrapes.

Water and sound. Water uses the same potential-flow added mass as the glass, inside the bowl's true shape. A bowl's walls move most near the rim, so water lowers its pitch much less than it lowers a glass's. At a few hundred hertz and tens of microns of wall motion, the waterline sees several g, which is enough for Faraday ripples and spray. Each mode pair is split by the hammering asymmetry, so the rotating quadrupole beats ("wah-wah") at a fixed listener.
